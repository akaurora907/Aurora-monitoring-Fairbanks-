✨ Upcoming astronomical events

Events relevant to Fairbanks and Interior Alaska. Dates are local Alaska dates. Meteor shower rates are approximate and actual viewing depends on sky conditions.

☄️ Geminid meteor shower

December 13–14, 2026

One of the year's strongest meteor showers. Moon illumination is approximately 15%, making for favorable dark-sky conditions.

☄️ Ursid meteor shower

December 21–22, 2026

Active around the December solstice. A bright Moon, approximately 89% illuminated, will interfere with fainter meteors.

☄️ Quadrantid meteor shower

January 3–4, 2027

A short, sometimes intense peak. Moon illumination is approximately 18%, which is favorable for viewing.

☄️ Lyrid meteor shower

April 21–22, 2027

The full Moon will create substantial interference, making the fainter meteors harder to see.

☄️ Eta Aquariid meteor shower

May 5–6, 2027

Very favorable Moon conditions, with approximately 2% illumination. The radiant's low position can limit viewing from Fairbanks.

☄️ Perseid meteor shower

August 12–13, 2027

A popular annual shower. Approximately 79% lunar illumination may make faint meteors harder to spot.

🌘 Penumbral lunar eclipse

August 16–17, 2027

Listed as visible from Fairbanks. A penumbral eclipse produces subtle shading across the Moon. This is not a total lunar eclipse or a blood moon.

🌕 Total lunar eclipse

December 31, 2028

The next listed total lunar eclipse visible from Fairbanks. Total lunar eclipses are the events commonly called blood moons.

Understanding the readings

Bz: Sustained negative values are generally favorable. Around -5 nT is useful; -10 nT or lower can be especially favorable.

By: Helps describe magnetic-field orientation. Its effect depends on other solar-wind and magnetospheric conditions.

Bt: Total magnetic-field strength. Stronger fields can be more influential, especially when Bz is southward.

Solar wind speed: Faster solar wind can increase energy transfer into Earth's magnetosphere.

Density: Describes the number of solar-wind protons. Higher density can increase dynamic pressure, but is not automatically better.

Kp: A global three-hour index. It is not a direct measure of visibility in Fairbanks.

OVATION: A model forecast, not a guarantee of visible aurora at your location.

AE: A measure of auroral electrojet activity. AL becoming more negative can help identify substorm development.
